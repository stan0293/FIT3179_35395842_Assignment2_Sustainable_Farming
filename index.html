Section 1: Australia’s Farming Landscape

Agriculture occupies more than half of the mapped area

Agricultural uses account for 55.5% of the total mapped area across the 57 NRM regions, approximately 426.6 million hectares. Non-agricultural uses account for the remaining 44.5%.

This provides context for the scale of farming. Land area alone does not measure sustainability.

WA: Western Australia; NT: Northern Territory; SA: South Australia; QLD: Queensland; NSW: New South Wales; VIC: Victoria; TAS: Tasmania; ACT: Australian Capital Territory. Dark outlines are derived from the mapped NRM regions grouped by state or territory.

Farming occupies an uneven share of the landscape

South West Queensland has the highest agricultural land share among the 57 mapped regions, at 92.2%. Southern Gulf follows at 90.3%, while Western, New South Wales, records 90.0%.

Agriculture includes cropping, horticulture and livestock production, including grazing. A higher agricultural share does not necessarily mean more sustainable farming.

Livestock production dominates agricultural land use

Livestock production occupies 49.8% of the total mapped area, followed by cropping at 5.6% and horticulture at 0.1%. Nature conservation is the largest non-agricultural category, at 39.1%.

These are shares of all mapped area, not just agricultural land. A small land share does not imply a small water requirement or environmental impact.
